# Writing for the people who hire

The reader that matters is a recruiter or hiring manager who gives a post about six seconds, and who often arrives by search, not by the feed.

## How they find a post
- They search by job title, by tool and by domain: "Power BI", "PMO", "supply chain analyst", "SAP". The site matches the text of the post and its hashtags.
- So every post names the tools and the kind of work in plain words, with official spelling, and carries the hashtag of each tool it names.
- Use the titles from `PROFILE.md` naturally in the text now and then ("as a business analyst, I..."). Never stuff keywords: a line of keywords reads as spam to the same recruiter.

## What they look for in six seconds
1. Role: what does this person do?
2. Scale: how many people, projects, customers, how much money or time?
3. Ownership: what did they build, run or decide, and what belonged to someone else?
4. Outcome: what changed because of it? A number if there is one.
5. Tools: which ones, used for what.

A post does not need all five, but a reader should be able to answer at least three from the post alone.

## The proof line
Somewhere in the first third, one sentence with a number and a verb of ownership: "I built the stock report three warehouses used, and late orders fell from 12% to 4% in a quarter." It is the line a recruiter will repeat to the hiring manager.

## What helps
- A small concrete case beats a general lesson.
- Tech news earns its place when it is tied to a tool or practice the person uses and ends in what they did with it. News alone shows reading, not ability.
- Roughly one post in three shows the work itself: a redacted dashboard, a before and after, a process on one page. Fictional data is labeled as fictional.
- Showing the limits of a role ("the owner decided; I brought the data") reads as senior, not weak.
- Posts in the language of the market the person is targeting.

## What hurts
- Any line about looking for work, unless the person asked for it.
- Complaints about an employer, a boss or a client.
- Confidential numbers, client names, internal documents.
- Claims the person could not defend in an interview. Every sentence is a possible interview question.
- Engagement bait ("comment YES") and humblebrag.

## Comments are also read by recruiters
A comment on someone else's post appears with the person's headline next to it. A sharp, specific comment on a mid-size author's post is often read by more of the right people than the person's own post.

## The profile is the landing page
A good post sends the recruiter to the profile. If the headline has no searchable title, the About opens with adjectives, or Featured is empty, say so in the weekly summary until it is fixed. Never change the profile without the person's approval for that exact change.

## Measure the goal
Impressions measure the post. Profile views, search appearances, recruiter messages and invitations received measure the goal. Report the second group first.
