# The interview

Do it one role at a time. Few questions per message. "I don't remember" is a valid answer: what is not confirmed is not used.

## A. Profile and goal (once)
1. Which roles do you want to be found for? Which market: local, international, both?
2. If a recruiter typed three words to find someone like you, what would they be? Which tools?
3. Which languages should the posts alternate between?
4. Is there an employer, a person or a topic to keep out, or to use sparingly?
5. Is anyone at your current employer not supposed to see this as job hunting?
6. Visual identity: colors, typeface, anything you already use.
7. Which days and what time should a run be ready for you to approve?

## B. Each role: open questions first
1. What was the job, who asked for it, when and for how long?
2. Who used what you produced, and what did they decide with it?
3. How many people, projects, customers, how much money? Rough is fine; say when you are unsure.
4. Which tools?
5. One situation you remember well, with a beginning, a middle and an end.
6. Can I name the company and the clients in a public post?

## C. Each role: the activity checklist
Open questions miss things people did and forgot. Write 15 to 20 typical activities for that role, grouped, and ask the person to answer by number with a note. Example for an operations analyst:

Requests and planning
1. Turn a manager's request into a written brief.
2. Decide the order of work when everything is urgent.

Tracking
3. Chase deadlines with each team.
4. Check that reported status matched what was actually delivered.
5. Flag work at risk before the review meeting.

Suppliers and money
6. Collect and compare supplier quotes.
7. Track each project's budget against what was spent.

Data
8. Clean the data before building each report.
9. Agree with leadership which indicators to follow.
10. Present the numbers in person.

Tools
11. Automate a manual task.
12. Train colleagues to use the tool.

Record the answers with their limits, in the person's words:
- "Yes, a lot" -> did it directly.
- "With the team" / "with the owner" -> shared; say "we" or name who led.
- "The owner did; I sat in" -> do NOT claim it.
- No answer -> not confirmed.

## D. Voice (ongoing)
- Write down phrases exactly as the person says them during the interview.
- Ask for one or two texts they wrote themselves, or a two-minute voice note telling a story; write from their words.
- Every time they edit a draft, store the before, the after and the rule it implies.

## E. Follow-ups
When a text later needs a detail that is missing, ask one short closed question ("At X, did you also do Y? How?") and wait. Add the answer to `FACTS.md` so it is never asked twice.
